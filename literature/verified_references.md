### B2. Published bounds on central or cavity redshift (lapse)

I found **no published inequality of the form α_center ≥ 1 − 2M/R**, or any other *lower* bound on the central lapse (i.e. an upper bound on central time dilation), that holds under ordinary energy conditions. The literature points the other way: the bounds below are lower bounds on central dilation, and explicit constructions reach arbitrarily large central redshift.

1. **Maximum principle (SEC) ⇒ the centre is at least as dilated as the surface.** J. Guven, N. Ó Murchadha, "Bounds on 2m/R for static spherical objects", Phys. Rev. D 60, 084020 (1999), [10.1103/PhysRevD.60.084020](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.084020), [gr-qc/9903067](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9903067), Sec. II: ΔN = 4π(ρ + tr S)N. "If the strong energy condition is satisfied ... ΔN ≥ 0 ... Thus the solution cannot possess an interior maximum. N falls towards the center." Hence N_c ≤ N(R) = sqrt(1−2M/R). For a hollow shell, the cavity lapse equals the lapse at the inner wall.
2. **Buchdahl central-lapse inequality (same paper, Sec. V, Eqs. 46–51).** For ρ ≥ 0, ρ' ≤ 0 and tangential stress ≤ radial stress, they show (N'/R)' ≤ 0, so N₀ ≥ N_c + (m₀/R₀³)∫R dℓ with ∫R dℓ ≥ ½(1 − (1−2m₀/R₀)^{1/2}) R₀³/m₀.
   - Combining these with N₀ = sqrt(1−2M/R) (arith., checked to give equality for uniform density): **N_c ≤ (3/2)·sqrt(1−2M/R) − 1/2.**
   - N_c ≥ 0 then gives 2M/R ≤ 8/9.
   - This is again an *upper* bound on the central lapse (a minimum central dilation), saturated by the uniform-density star. Buchdahl (1959) states the same family of inequalities in its abstract: "inequalities comparing the boundary value of g44 with ... the ratio of the central energy density to the central pressure".
3. **No upper bound on central redshift in general:**
   - **J. V. Narlikar, P. K. Das, "On the maximization of the central gravitational red-shift of a Schwarzschild sphere with a given surface gravitational red-shift", Gen. Relativ. Gravit. 7, 769–780 (1976), [10.1007/BF00778757](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00778757).** Abstract, via the [IAS repository record](https://repository.ias.ac.in/41218): in Bondi-type core–envelope models, arbitrarily large central redshifts are possible for sufficiently large radii if the surface redshift is ≤ ~4.77. With sound speed ≤ c there is a limiting surface redshift ≈ 1.71; at that limit z_c is finite and unique, and below it z_c can again be arbitrarily large. (PARTIALLY VERIFIED: abstract summary only.)
   - **G. S. Bisnovatyi-Kogan, K. S. Thorne, "Relativistic gas spheres and clusters of point masses with arbitrarily large central redshifts: can they be stable?", ApJ 160, 875 (1970), [10.1086/150478](https://doi.org/10.1086/150478).** Abstract (OpenAlex): models of Bisnovatyi-Kogan & Zel'dovich "for which the red shift from the center to infinity is arbitrarily large ... we prove that the gas spheres are, indeed, stable—at least against radial perturbations." For clusters the result is inconclusive, but "probably they are stable".
   - Contrasting rigorous result: M. Hadžić, Z. Lin, G. Rein, "Stability and instability of self-gravitating relativistic matter distributions", Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 241, 1 (2021), [10.1007/s00205-021-01647-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00205-021-01647-2), [1810.00809](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00809). Within one-parameter families parameterized by central redshift κ (Einstein–Vlasov and Einstein–Euler), the steady states are linearly unstable when κ is sufficiently large.
   - G. S. Bisnovatyi-Kogan, M. Merafina, ApJ 653, 1445 (2006), [10.1086/508794](https://doi.org/10.1086/508794), [astro-ph/0603398](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0603398): dynamical versus thermodynamical stability of relativistic clusters.
4. **Surface/compactness bounds under energy conditions.** These fix the *surface* lapse, and for thin shells also the cavity lapse.
   - **Thin shell, flat interior, DEC:** 2M/R ≤ 24/25, so the cavity lapse is ≥ 1/5 and the **maximum cavity dilation is 5** (arith.).
     - Sources: Frauendiener–Hoenselaers–Konrad (1990, #41), as reported by Andréasson (2007).
     - Re-derivation: J. Arrechea, C. Barceló, G. García-Moreno, J. Polo-Gómez, "Beyond Buchdahl's limit: bilayered stars and thin-shell configurations", Phys. Rev. D 111, 124017 (2025), [10.1103/dqbd-9z5f](https://doi.org/10.1103/dqbd-9z5f), [2411.14018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14018), Eqs. 69–72: σ ≥ p̃_t ⇒ C = 2M/R ≤ 24/25 = 0.96. Their words: "the distributional tangential pressures of the shell grow without upper bounds as this limit is approached".
   - **Andréasson (2007, CMP 274, 399):** for shells with p + q ≤ Ωρ and R₁/R₀ → 1, lim sup 2M/R₁ ≤ ((2Ω+1)²−1)/(2Ω+1)². Ω = 1 (Vlasov) gives 8/9; Ω = 2 gives 24/25.
   - **Andréasson (2008, JDE 245, 2243):** ρ ≥ 0, p ≥ 0, p + 2p_T ≤ Ωρ ⇒ sup 2m/r ≤ ((1+2Ω)²−1)/(1+2Ω)², and this is sharp. "Any model with p ≥ 0 which satisfies the dominant energy condition satisfies the hypotheses with Ω = 3", giving 48/49, a surface lapse ≥ 1/7 and a surface dilation ≤ 7 (arith.).
   - **H. Bondi, "Massive spheres in general relativity", Proc. R. Soc. A 282, 303 (1964), [10.1098/rspa.1964.0234](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1964.0234).** Isotropic case. Abstract: "if the density is nowhere negative then u < 0.485 ..., while if in addition the density is nowhere less than three times the pressure and nowhere increasing outwards, then u < 0.319" (u = m/r). Arrechea et al. give the first bound as 2M/R < 12√2 − 16 ≈ 0.971.
   - **B. V. Ivanov, "Maximum bounds on the surface redshift of anisotropic stars", Phys. Rev. D 65, 104011 (2002), [10.1103/PhysRevD.65.104011](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.65.104011), [gr-qc/0201090](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0201090).** Abstract: "the surface redshift cannot exceed the values 3.842 or 5.211 when the tangential pressure satisfies the strong or the dominant energy condition, respectively". These assume positive radial pressure and decreasing density, per Arrechea et al.
   - P. Karageorgis, J. G. Stalker, "Sharp bounds on 2m/r for static spherical objects", Class. Quantum Grav. 25, 195021 (2008), [10.1088/0264-9381/25/19/195021](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/25/19/195021), [0707.3632](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.3632).
   - A. Alho, J. Natário, P. Pani, G. Raposo, "Compactness bounds in general relativity", Phys. Rev. D 106, L041502 (2022), [10.1103/PhysRevD.106.L041502](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.106.L041502), [2202.00043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00043). For elastic matter, subluminal waves give GM/R ≈ 0.462 (2M/R ≈ 0.924), and adding radial stability gives GM/R ≈ 0.389 (2M/R ≈ 0.778, surface dilation ≈ 2.1, arith.).
   - H. Andréasson, S. Calogero, "Spherically symmetric steady states of John elastic bodies in general relativity", Class. Quantum Grav. 31, 165008 (2014), [10.1088/0264-9381/31/16/165008](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/31/16/165008), [1403.2565](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.2565): "gravitational redshift can be very large (z ≈ 2.8) without violating the dominant energy condition".
5. **Charged-dust exception:** Bonnor & Wickramasuriya (1975) and Lemos–Weinberg (2004) give arbitrarily large redshifts with ordinary (positive-energy) but exactly extremal charged dust (#16, #17). The obstacles are charge neutrality and Schwinger discharge (#6, #7), not the energy conditions.

- NGC 1365: "rapidly spinning". G. Risaliti et al., Nature 494, 449 (2013), [10.1038/nature11938](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11938), [1302.7002](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7002). The abstract gives no number; the often-quoted a > 0.84 was NOT VERIFIED.
- Review: C. S. Reynolds, "Observational constraints on black hole spin", ARA&A 59, 117 (2021), [10.1146/annurev-astro-112420-035022](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-astro-112420-035022), [2011.08948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08948). "Many accreting supermassive black holes are found to be rapidly spinning", with more slowly spinning ones at the highest masses.
- Caution on reflection-based spins: J. A. García, R. Connors, L. W. Brenneman, J. F. Steiner, Galaxies 14, 71 (2026), [10.3390/galaxies14040071](https://doi.org/10.3390/galaxies14040071), which proposes robust/provisional quality tiers.
- Stellar-mass record: Cygnus X-1 has a* > 0.9985 (3σ) by continuum fitting. X. Zhao et al., ApJ 908, 117 (2021), [10.3847/1538-4357/abbcd6](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/abbcd6), [2102.09093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09093). Its mass is 21.2 ± 2.2 M_⊙: J. C. A. Miller-Jones et al., Science 371, 1046 (2021), [10.1126/science.abb3363](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abb3363).
- No measured spin comes close to Interstellar's 1 − a ~ 10⁻¹⁴. Thorne's 0.998 equilibrium limit (ApJ 191, 507, 1974) remains the standard astrophysical ceiling.
